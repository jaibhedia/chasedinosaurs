## 1. Key facts

- **Deadline: Saturday, November 7th 2026 at 09:00 am IST.** "Late submissions will not be
  accepted."
- **Submit** through the Hacker Dashboard form: project title, description and a link to the
  repository. A demo video is optional but strongly encouraged. On the last step you pick up
  to 3 partner prizes.
- **Stake.** Each participant stakes ETH to secure their spot (connect MetaMask, Coinbase
  Wallet or Rainbow on the Hacker Dashboard). It comes back within about 4 weeks after the
  event if you submitted a project, so keep the Ethereum address on the dashboard correct.
- **Teams.** Up to 5 people. "Hackathon applications are considered at the individual level;
  as such, each team member must be accepted and stake individually." Solo is fine. To join
  a team, use "Join Team" with the leader's team token. To start one, use "Project", then
  name and describe it.
- **Contacts:**
  - hello@ethglobal.com: general help, staking problems or hardship.
  - incidents@ethglobal.com: code of conduct. Anonymous reports are OK.
  - Discord: official announcements, private event channels, #find-a-team and
    #mentorship-help.

## 2. Tracks

**Classic "From Scratch":** "your project must be started and developed during the
hackathon. You may use public libraries or boilerplate, but pre-existing project-specific
code, designs, or assets are not allowed." "Projects built before the event may still
participate but won't qualify for partner prizes or the Finalist category."

**Continuity ("Extend Open Source" / "Ship a Feature"):** "you may build on an existing
codebase according to the rules of that track. In those tracks you can bring an existing
open-source repository or extend an existing private/commercial product, but the work
submitted for judging must include new features or functionality developed during the
hackathon." "Continuity submissions must clearly document pre-existing work and include new
features or functionality developed during the hackathon; eligibility for partner prizes may
vary by event and partner."

"Only the work you complete during the hackathon will be evaluated. Judges will be looking
for creativity, functionality, and the quality of your final product."

> **What this means for Chase Dinosaurs:** the repo dates from 2026-06-20, so it's a
> Continuity entry. "Ship a Feature" fits a proprietary product, and it was the track chosen
> at ETHOnline. Check each target partner's rules for whether they accept Continuity entries.

## 3. Version control

- "Use version control to track your code during the event. Submissions with large single
  commits or missing histories may be disqualified, as it's important to show your progress
  throughout the hackathon."
- "You're welcome to use open-source libraries and starter kits to kickstart your project,
  but be transparent."
- "Your submission should include a GitHub Repo, Figma files, or equivalent, proving the work
  was done during the hackathon. Clearly distinguish between what's new and what's reused."

## 4. AI tools

"The use of AI tools (e.g., ChatGPT, Claude Code, GitHub Copilot, Cursor, etc.) is generally
permitted, but with the following guidelines to ensure transparency and integrity:"

- **Attribution:** "Clearly document in your submission where and how AI tools were used in
  the project. This includes specifying which parts of the code, specific files, or assets
  were generated or assisted by AI."
- **Involvement:** "AI tools should be used to assist your development process, not to create
  the entire project. Submissions that rely entirely on AI without meaningful contributions
  from team members may not be eligible for partner prizes or finalist consideration."
- **Spec-driven development:** "Using spec-driven workflows (e.g., OpenSpec, Kiro, spec-kit)
  is permitted. If you use one, you must include all spec files, prompts, and planning
  artifacts in your submission repository. Judges need to see the full picture of how you
  directed the AI, not just the generated output."

## 5. Submitting

You choose one of two options:

1. **Finalist and Partner Prizes.** "Opting for this will require you to present your project
   to judges in the Finalist judging session." Presenting at partner booths is optional
   (feedback and networking); partners judge from what you submit.
2. **Partner Prizes only.** "For each partner prize, you'll need to explain how you've used
   or integrated their tools, provide feedback, and share relevant comments." Booth visits
   are optional here too.

Partner prizes:

- "You can select up to 3 Partner Prizes to apply for. If a partner has multiple tracks, you
  can be eligible for all of them while only counting as 1 Partner Prize."
- "Please ensure to select these prizes carefully as it is the only way for partners to
  assess your project."

Demo video: "While optional, we strongly encourage you to include a 2-4 minute demo video
showcasing your project. This video will be featured on the ETHGlobal Showcase." See §7.

## 6. Judging

**The Finalist session.** Its details appear on your Hacker Dashboard after you submit. Go to
the judging check-in table first. "Each team has 7 minutes to present: 4 minutes for the
demo, followed by 3 minutes for Q&A with the judges." Common questions:

- What inspired your project?
- What tools did you use, and why?
- What challenges did you solve, and how?

**Criteria:**

| Criterion | What judges ask |
|---|---|
| Technicality | How complex is the problem you're addressing, and how sophisticated is your solution? |
| Originality | Is your project introducing a new idea or creatively solving an existing problem? |
| Practicality | How complete and functional is your project? Could it be used by its target audience today? |
| Usability (UI/UX/DX) | How intuitive is your project? Have you made it easy for users to interact with your solution? |
| WOW factor | Does your project leave a lasting impression? The catch-all for anything unique or impressive. |

**Partner judging** is based on the materials you submit. Winners are announced at the
closing ceremony. "Check partner-specific rules for any additional requirements." The FAQ
also says to present at partner booths (see the partner map), while the submission
guidelines call booth visits optional. Go if there's time, because it's free feedback.

## 7. Demo video

- **2 to 4 minutes.** Shorter or longer videos are rejected at upload.
- Speak clearly and don't rush. Avoid background noise and echo.
- Keep the introduction under 20 seconds.
- Show the project working, and edit out any waiting.
- Use slides to summarise, with no more than 4 bullet points each.

Mistakes that get a video sent back:

- 🚨 Exported below 720p (the upload fails)
- 🚨 Longer than 4 minutes (the upload fails)
- 🚨 Sped up to fit the time limit
- 🚨 Music with on-screen text instead of you talking
- 🚨 Recorded on a mobile phone
- 🚨 A text-to-speech or AI voiceover

## 8. During the event

- **Code of conduct:** be respectful and inclusive. Harassment can lead to disqualification.
  Report to incidents@ethglobal.com.
- **Mentors:** they have the "Mentor" role on Discord (ask in #mentorship-help) and wear
  high-visibility jackets in person.
- **Feedback sessions** follow the website schedule. Bring a demo, Figma, slides or just
  ideas.

## 9. After the event

- **Stake.** It's returned within about 4 weeks if you submitted. Check the Ethereum address
  on the Hacker Dashboard.
- **Survey.** About 5 minutes, in the Hacker Dashboard.
- **Spotlight.** ETHGlobal features early-stage, user-ready projects so they can get user
  feedback and an audience. Apply after the event.
- **More events.** Follow the mailing list, Discord, X and the events calendar.
